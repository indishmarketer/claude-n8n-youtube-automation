# Script Style

This file controls script structure. `example-scripts.md` controls voice. Both matter.

## Structure template

This is the template that works on the channel. Follow it unless the topic clearly needs something else.

### 1. Proof hook (first 15 seconds)

Open with the result, not the promise. Show that the thing already worked before explaining it.

Weak: "Today I'm going to show you how to automate YouTube videos."
Strong: "This video was written, voiced and uploaded without me touching an editor. Here's the whole system."

Self-referential proof works best. If the video is about a system, use the system as the proof.

### 2. Context (30 to 60 seconds)

Why this exists. What was broken before. Keep it short and real.

### 3. Numbered steps

Say the number out loud. "Step one is..." not "First, we'll look at...". Viewers use numbered steps as mental checkpoints and retention holds better.

Each step:
- State what it is
- State why it matters
- State exactly what to do
- Move on

### 4. The part nobody shows you

Somewhere past the halfway mark, use a variation of "here's the part nobody shows you". This is where the real failure, the real cost, or the real limitation goes.

Examples of what belongs here:
- The plan tier that broke the whole thing
- The feature that does not exist despite what the docs imply
- The version that got deleted after two weeks of work
- The actual monthly cost

This section is why people trust the video. Do not skip it and do not soften it.

### 5. Current state

Where the system is right now. What is still broken. What is next.

### 6. CTA

One CTA. Specific. Whatever came in the payload.

## Length

Long form. If the topic supports twelve minutes, write twelve minutes. A five minute cut of a twelve minute topic gets rejected.

Rough guide: 130 to 150 spoken words per minute.

## Voice rules

- First person, present tense where natural
- Active voice
- Short sentences. Vary the length so it does not read like a list.
- Contractions are fine. This is speech.
- No em dashes, ever
- No formal or literary phrasing
- No AI filler: "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "unlock", "leverage", "game changer", "revolutionize", "seamlessly"

## Using reference material

If a reference script or video is shared as a style example, copy the **structural technique only**. Never copy its claims, its stories, its numbers or its specific narrative devices. The script must stick strictly to the operator's real story.
